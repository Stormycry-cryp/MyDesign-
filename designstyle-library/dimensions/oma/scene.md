# Scene

## Observed
- Product scene: architecture, interior-design, studio
- Audience: architecture studios, project-led portfolios, editorial practice sites
- Page scope: Official homepage for the architecture practice; homepage-led reference with project/news modules.
- Community signal: Selected in the 2026-06-08 curated daily candidate batch for DesignStyle and explicitly confirmed by the user for ingestion despite a borderline preflight score because the first viewport remained distinctive and captureable.
- Essence: OMA uses an oversized typographic shell over a full-bleed project image, then anchors the featured story with a stark white information slab and red-accent editorial card. The transferable idea is not luxury polish but a blunt institutional-news rhythm: giant navigation words, one dominant image, and one sharply framed project announcement.
- When to use:
  - Use when a studio, practice, or cultural organization needs a homepage that feels like a live bulletin board rather than a polished marketing funnel.
  - Use for project-led portfolios where large headlines and image-first hierarchy should do most of the work.
  - Use when the interface can tolerate sparse controls and high visual confidence.
- When not to use:
  - Do not use for conversion-heavy SaaS, ecommerce, or onboarding flows that need layered explanation and repeated CTAs.
  - Do not use when the product requires warm tone, soft surfaces, or dense utility navigation.
  - Do not use this reference to justify weak content architecture; its success depends on strong imagery and short news framing.

## Inference
- Borrow:
  - Borrow the confidence of giant shell typography and the discipline of showing one project at a time.
  - Borrow the editorial use of date + headline inside a bordered information slab.
  - Borrow the image-led hierarchy where navigation feels like part of the art direction.
